Hydro Jetting for Genesee Street Hill Properties

The village historian identifies Greek Revival homes on Genesee Street Hill as part of the Fayetteville Historic District. Homes of that style are old by any standard, and few have kept the plumbing they were built with.

A historic house often holds a layered plumbing history. The main lateral may have been replaced in one decade, a bathroom added in another, and a kitchen rerouted in a third. The age of the structure tells you very little about the age or material of the line beneath it.

Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to scour grease, scale and roots from a sound pipe wall. For a historic home, the inspection comes first, and a weakened or cracked section may need repair before it needs cleaning.

Frequently Asked Questions About Hydro Jetting in Genesee Street Hill

Is my home in the Fayetteville Historic District?

The village can confirm it. If it is, ask whether planned exterior work needs review.

Does a Greek Revival house mean old plumbing?

Not necessarily. Many historic homes carry newer sewer work, so the line's material and condition come from records and an inspection.

Is high pressure risky in an old house?

If the pipe is cracked or weak, it can be. That is why the camera comes first.

Can roots from old trees clog my line?

Yes, if they have entered a joint. The camera can confirm it, and jetting can clear them from a sound pipe.

Does cleaning fix a damaged pipe?

No. It clears an obstruction. Damage needs a repair assessment.

What details should I give when I request service?

List the affected fixtures, when the problem started, and anything that changed around that time. Mention any past cleanings or repairs, and where the cleanout is if you know.

How is jetting different from snaking?

A snake opens a path through a blockage, while jetting scours the pipe wall with high-pressure water. For residue that keeps causing repeat clogs, jetting addresses what snaking leaves behind, when the pipe's condition allows.

Do I need an inspection before jetting?

Yes. The cause of the blockage decides the method, and a cracked or weak pipe can be made worse by high pressure. Inspection first is the rule for any property.
